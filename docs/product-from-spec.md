# Product Proposal

**One-line description:** A neighborhood mutual-aid marketplace whose payment disputes are settled in a dual-custody courtroom using hourly-notarized bearer keys, signed witnesses, two kinds of blind-signature race, and a double-verifying arbiter.

**Primary user:** A neighbor in a small local community who lists goods and skills in the marketplace, receives courier deliveries, and occasionally has to prove in the courtroom that a payment or reservation was valid.

## Flow

1. **The pool is funded.** A five-sub-wallet grant committee (1) votes to release community savings into the courtroom's Lightning channel (2). Funds move only on a majority of the five revocable keys, so no single neighbor can empty the pool. That channel becomes the court's permanent savings exhibit.

2. **Neighbors trade.** Sellers post each listing as a kind-1 note carrying a category `t` tag and a seller `p` tag (10). Buyers filter locally and reserve a listing with a zap. Small "coffee money" payments are held as ecash in the court (2); the larger savings stay in the channel.

3. **Keys rotate and are witnessed.** The locksmith rotates the ecash bearer secret every hour, and a traffic-light oracle notarizes each rotation with a since-filtered signal note proving exactly when the old pubkey was revoked (8). This matters because the court's `get_info` testimony about the ecash expires hourly while the channel exhibit is immortal — so every disputed zap receipt must be tied to the bearer key that was valid at the moment it was signed.

4. **Deliveries are authorized remotely.** When a courier arrives, the doorbell (5) unlocks only if the delivery's payment proof checks against a block the user's own node confirmed minutes ago. That recent-block check only works because the resilient network interface (7) keeps channels to newly discovered relays and a fresh local view when the primary relay is down.

5. **Bigger deals use timed escrow.** For a larger listing, buyer and seller agree on a note and the connection expires at `expires_at` the moment a zap receipt matching that note confirms the payment milestone (12). The agreed note is signed and chained into the courtroom's e-tagged chain (4), so the receipt is admissible evidence later.

6. **Disputes go to the courtroom.** When two buyers claim one reservation, or a bearer signature is challenged, the court runs a race. A "who paid first" dispute uses the village mint race (6): each neighbor's blind-signed token lands as a `payment_received` in an isolated sub-wallet, and the winner gets their ecash reissued with a fresh blinding factor nobody can link. A "whose blind signature is genuine" dispute uses the unblinding-first race (11): the winner is the sub-wallet whose blind signature unblinds first, and the loser's tokens are melted into a fossil-record event on a censoring relay. The blinding magician (3) supplies and reblinds the secret, crossfading it through inbound and outbound capacity as a trick, and only reveals the key by signing a kind-1 event after the `payment_sent` prediction lands — so the reveal itself is timed evidence, not an early giveaway.

7. **One result is made canonical.** The two races would otherwise produce competing claims. The race arbiter (14) double-verifies every incoming signature — against the oracle-notarized current bearer pubkey (8) and the signed witness notes in the courtroom chain (4) — emits a signed alert when a request pattern looks anomalous (for the security team's audit trail), and signs one canonical race result that settles features 6 and 11. That signed result is the court's decision.

8. **The system audits itself.** A bug-bounty escape room (9) hides a PSBT as the puzzle; a hunter who solves it receives an offline bearer token, and the exit invoice is paid by the first node to independently verify the block (which again needs the fresh view from 7). A bug-bounty watchtower (13) punishes cheating channels by day and, at midnight, swaps the recovered sats into blind-signed IOUs that a federation of guardians cannot link back to the exploit report; those recovered sats refill the bounty pool. The arbiter's anomaly alerts (14) point the watchtower at the channels worth watching.

## Feature-to-usage mapping

| ID | Feature | How the product actually uses it |
|----|---------|----------------------------------|
| 1 | Multisig-style grant committee | The five revocable keys gate every release of community savings into the courtroom channel; a majority vote is required before any grant, escrow, or bounty is funded. Without it there is no treasury and the channel exhibit in 2 stays empty. |
| 2 | Dual-custody courtroom | The venue for all disputes. Pooled savings sit in the immortal channel exhibit while daily coffee money sits in ecash whose `get_info` testimony expires hourly. This custody split is what forces every claim to be anchored in signed, notarized evidence rather than a live balance. |
| 3 | Blinding magician | Produces and reblinds the race secret, moving it through inbound/outbound channel capacity, and reveals the key by signing a kind-1 event only after the `payment_sent` prediction lands. It is the source of the fresh, unlinkable blinding factor in 6 and the timed reveal the court admits as evidence. |
| 4 | Favorite-color state query | A witness proves they know the wallet's current mood by signing a Nostr note that is chained with an `e` tag into the courtroom thread. That signed note is the admissible identity/liveness proof the arbiter (14) checks before trusting testimony. |
| 5 | Courier-access doorbell | Unlocks for a remote courier only when the payment proof is checked against a block the user's own node confirmed minutes ago. It is the verifiable authorization step that turns a marketplace reservation (10) into a completed delivery, and its decisions can be challenged in court (2). |
| 6 | Village mint "who paid first" race | The court's race for reservation conflicts: each neighbor's blind-signed token arrives as a `payment_received` in an isolated sub-wallet, and the winner's ecash is reissued with a fresh blinding factor nobody can link. It decides "who paid first" and is one of the two results the arbiter (14) reconciles. |
| 7 | Resilient network interface | Keeps channels to newly discovered relays so the local view of blocks and notes stays fresh when the primary relay fails. The doorbell's recent-block check (5), the recency of courtroom notes (4), and the arbiter's verification (14) all depend on it. |
| 8 | Notarized hourly key rotation | The locksmith's hourly rotation is notarized by the traffic-light oracle's since-filtered signal note, proving when the old pubkey was revoked. This is the star exhibit for "was this zap receipt signed by the right bearer?" and tells the arbiter (14) which pubkey was valid at signing time. |
| 9 | Bug-bounty escape room | The community's security test: a PSBT is the puzzle, an offline bearer token is the prize, and the exit invoice is paid by the first node to independently verify the block. It gives the recovered sats from 13 a payout path and relies on 7 for the first-verifier race. |
| 10 | Neighborhood marketplace | The user-facing surface: listings are kind-1 notes with `t` category and `p` seller tags, buyers filter locally and reserve with zaps. Its zaps create the `payment_received` events for races (6, 11), the milestones for escrow (12), and the payments the doorbell (5) checks. |
| 11 | Unblinding-first race | The court's authenticity/double-spend race: the sub-wallet whose blind signature unblinds first wins, and the loser's tokens are melted into a fossil-record event on a censoring relay. It decides "whose blind signature is genuine" alongside 6. |
| 12 | Timed-release document escrow | On a larger listing, the connection auto-expires at `expires_at` as soon as a zap receipt matching the agreed note confirms the milestone. Because that agreed note lives in the courtroom's e-tagged chain (4), expiry is provable and removes a whole class of "did you deliver?" disputes. |
| 13 | Bug-bounty watchtower | Punishes cheating channels by day and at midnight swaps recovered sats into blind-signed IOUs a federation of guardians cannot link to the exploit report. It protects the court's channel savings (2), funds the escape-room pool (9), and acts on the arbiter's anomaly alerts (14). |
| 14 | Race arbiter | The fraud-detection trust anchor: double-verifies every incoming app signature, emits a signed alert on anomalous request patterns for the security audit trail, and signs one canonical race result, resolving the competing claims of 6 and 11 so the court has a single decision. |

## More than the sum of its parts

The product is a chain of dependencies, not a pile of features. Remove the grant committee (1) and there is no pool to fund the courtroom channel (2). Remove the dual custody (2) and there is no venue where an immortal channel exhibit and hourly-expiring ecash testimony can be compared. Remove the notarized rotation (8) and nobody can tell which bearer key signed a zap receipt, so the arbiter (14) cannot double-verify, so the races (6, 11) have no canonical winner, so the court cannot decide, so marketplace disputes (10) stall and the doorbell (5) cannot authorize deliveries. Remove the resilient relays (7) and the doorbell's recent-block check and the arbiter's signature checks go stale on a relay failure. Remove the signed witness note (4) and witnesses can be impersonated; remove the magician (3) and the race secret has no producer, no fresh blinding factor, and no reveal timed to `payment_sent`. Remove either race and half of all disputes become undecidable; remove the arbiter and the two races issue two competing results. Remove the escrow (12) and every large listing becomes a manual court case; remove the doorbell and reservations cannot be proven delivered. Remove the watchtower (13) and channel cheating goes unpunished while whistleblowers are exposed and the bounty pool is unfunded; remove the escape room (9) and there is no way to test the system or pay the first independent verifier. Each strange piece has exactly one job that no other piece does, and the court is where all of them have to agree.
